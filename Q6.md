# Q6 — Street View OSINT: Where Is This? (2 marks)

## The Task

You're given a Street View panorama. Your job is to find where it was taken using only publicly available information: visual clues, road signs, architecture, vegetation, language, sun position, shadow direction — everything except the metadata.

Submit as: `Place, Country, Latitude, Longitude`  
**All-or-nothing:** place, country, AND coordinates must all be correct (within 100m). No partial credit.

---

## Approach

### 1. **Visual Reconnaissance (the first 2 minutes)**

Look at the Street View image and note:
- **Language on signs** — what script? (Latin, Cyrillic, Arabic, Chinese, Japanese, Korean, etc.)
- **Driving side** — cars on left = UK, India, Japan, Australia... cars on right = USA, Europe, most of world
- **Road markings & kerbs** — colour and style (yellow/red lines, white-edged kerbs, etc.)
- **Architecture** — building styles, roof shapes, materials
- **Vegetation** — tropical, temperate, deciduous, coniferous?
- **Utility poles** — design, cross-arms, insulators
- **License plates** — colour, shape, visible text
- **Climate indicators** — snow, desert, monsoon lushness

### 2. **Reverse Image Search (if you have a screenshot)**

- Right-click → "Search Image with Google"
- TinEye (https://tineye.com)
- Yandex (https://yandex.com/images)

Even a 10% match often reveals the city or landmark.

### 3. **Tool: Geo-Locations Reference**

Your friend built a handy reverse-geolocation tool:  
**https://hypemonk.github.io/Geo-locations/**

Use it to:
- Search for landmarks by name or country
- Verify coordinates for known places
- Cross-check driving side, utility pole styles by region

⭐ **Star the repo if it helps:** https://github.com/hypemonk/Geo-locations

### 4. **Narrow Down by Querying**

Once you have a country (or strong hypothesis):
- Google Maps Street View: search the landmark or general area
- Ctrl+F the landmark in the map UI
- Look for unique visual markers (signs, murals, building facades, park gates)

### 5. **Coordinate Verification**

Once you have lat/lon:
- Paste into Google Maps
- Zoom in on Street View
- Compare the panorama with what you see at that pin
- If it matches, you're done ✓

---

## Common Gotchas

- **Place vs. City:** If the image is at a park or monument, the grader usually wants the **park/monument name** (e.g., "Hiroshima Peace Memorial Park"), not just the city. But then also make sure the coordinates point to *that place*, not the city centre.
  - **Exception:** If the place name is not unique enough, fall back to the city name.
- **Exact naming:** The grader ignores case and punctuation, so "hiroshima, japan" and "Hiroshima Peace Memorial Park, Japan" both work if the place is correct. But the place must be *recognised* — typos or partial names fail.
- **Coordinate precision:** Within 100 metres. You don't need 6 decimal places; 2–3 decimals usually gets you there. Test by pinning in Google Maps.

## Example: Hiroshima Peace Memorial Park

**Visual clues:**
- Japanese script on signage
- Left-hand driving
- Arched gate with Japanese architecture
- Park setting with lush vegetation

**Search:** "Peace Memorial Park Japan" in Google Maps → Found.  
**Coordinates:** 34.3905, 132.4519  
**Submit:** `Hiroshima, Japan, 34.3905, 132.4519` ✅

(Some variants: "Hiroshima Peace Memorial Park, Japan" also accept, as long as the pin is within 100m.)

## Workflow

1. **Screenshot the Street View** (if allowed) or study it carefully.
2. **Note all visual clues** — language, driving side, signs, architecture.
3. **Make a country guess** (or narrow to 2–3 regions).
4. **Search Google Maps** for the landmark or area.
5. **Verify on Street View** at the pin location.
6. **Check coordinates** — within 100m is good.
7. **Submit:** `Place, Country, Lat, Lon`

---

## Pro Tips

- **Sun position:** If you see a clear shadow direction, the sun was in the opposite direction. At noon, sun is North in Southern Hemisphere, South in Northern. This can narrow down latitude.
- **Read every sign:** Even a blurry text on a store or billboard can be Googled for location.
- **Listen to the audio:** Street View sometimes has ambient audio — accents, traffic patterns, bells/horns unique to a region.
- **Unique landmarks:** If you spot anything unusual (a specific statue, mural, gate, building), search it directly. Chances are high it's been photographed and tagged on social media or travel blogs.
